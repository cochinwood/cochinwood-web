An Indian factory, not a UK stockist

Our group has pressed plywood in Perumbavoor, Kerala since 1986. The units are about 40 km from Cochin Port, so a container stuffs at the factory and gates in at the Vallarpadam terminal the same day. We sell FOB Cochin: we manufacture, pack, clear the goods for export and load them on board, and you or your forwarder carry the ocean leg to the UK port you nominate.

That is worth stating plainly, because it is the difference between buying from us and buying from a UK importer's yard. There is no British warehouse behind this page and no local stock. What you get instead is factory pricing, a specification agreed before the panels are pressed, and a loading plan built to the container's weight limit rather than to a merchant's pallet count.

The United Kingdom is one of several lanes we run out of Cochin. The rest are on the export hub, and the mechanics of an order — who does what, and when — are set out in how we handle an export order.

Origin data, certification and emission class

Buyers increasingly ask about origin before they ask about price, and the answer should arrive with the quote rather than after the shipment. For UK-bound orders we supply the same origin pack we prepare for European buyers: species and country of harvest for the timber in the panel, faces and cores both, and legality documentation for harvest and trade in the country of origin. Much of what we press is plantation timber — rubberwood above all — grown as an agricultural crop and replanted on a cycle, which traces to plots far more readily than mixed natural-forest material.

We do not hold a single certification that covers everything we sell; each mill holds its own licences. If your tender needs FSC chain of custody, a Declaration of Performance, or a stated formaldehyde class such as E1, tell us at enquiry. It decides which mill the order is placed with, and it cannot be added after the panels are pressed. Original certificates are submitted on request. The registrations you can verify yourself, without asking us, are listed on our company verification page.

On the conformity-marking route for panels sold into Great Britain for construction use: CE marking is still accepted, and not as an allowance with an expiry date on it — the government settled that on 2 September 2024, after the Morrell-Day review, and undertook to give at least two years’ notice before changing it. UKCA marking runs alongside as a valid parallel route rather than a replacement. Which route your project wants is a question for your own compliance adviser against the specification; tell us the answer at enquiry, because the Declaration of Performance and the declared formaldehyde class have to come from the mill that presses the panels.

Terms, currency and paperwork

Export quotes are issued in USD. FOB Cochin is our default term; a CIF figure to your nominated UK port is available on request, priced against current freight. We do not publish rates. Each quote is built against your thickness, size, grade and quantity, and the reply goes out within one business day.

Standard products ship against 50 percent advance with the balance before dispatch; custom specifications are 100 percent advance. We accept bank transfer (T/T). Every shipment carries our material guarantee: the panel that arrives matches the spec sheet quoted.

Plywood panels are processed wood and exempt from ISPM-15, so bare sheets need no heat-treatment stamp. Solid-wood packaging is treated: we heat-treat at registered facilities and stencil the IPPC mark, country code IN, treatment code HT and the registered facility number, which is the standard for exports to the EU, the UK and most other destinations we ship to.

Customs dutyThe UK Global Tariff (MFN) rate on HS 4412 plywood is 6% ad valorem, confirmed directly on HMRC’s own Trade Tariff tool for the 4412.33 and 4412.39 subheadings. The UK–India trade agreement (CETA) entered into force on 15 July 2026, and for one specific plywood code (4412 3400 10) the same tool shows a 0% preferential rate for India-origin goods, conditional on a valid certificate or statement of origin under the agreement’s rules of origin — without that proof, the 6% MFN rate is what customs will charge. Whether that 0% preference reaches every plywood subheading is not yet confirmed on the government’s own pages, so treat 6% as the safe planning figure and have your broker verify the live rate, CETA eligibility included, against CWI’s exact 10-digit code before final costing.
