Cochin Wood Industries is an Indian plywood factory. Our group has worked timber at Perumbavoor, Kerala since 1986, and the factories sit about 40 km from Cochin Port, where your container is stuffed and gated in. We hold no stock in the Caribbean, so what you buy is the panel at the factory gate, priced in US dollars.

Puerto Rico is one of several lanes we run into the Americas. The export hub covers the rest, and plywood export to the United States goes deeper on the formaldehyde question below.

Formaldehyde: confirm which regime your entry falls under

Buyers bringing plywood into Puerto Rico ask us the same certification question mainland buyers do. We will not tell you which regime applies to your entry — that is your customs broker's call, and it depends on the product type and its end use. What we will do is act on the answer.

If the answer is CARB or TSCA Title VI, name it at enquiry. It decides which mill your order is placed with, and it cannot be retrofitted after the panels are pressed. We make no blanket compliance claim: each mill holds its own BIS licence and its own test reports, and any certificate that applies to your order is identified on the quotation by holder, number and product scope, then released to verified buyers on request. A low-emission E1 glue line is available on Okoume-faced panels, but E1 and CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI are different regimes with different test methods, so confirm the exact class rather than treating one as a substitute. Our note on formaldehyde compliance for plywood exports sets out what to ask any supplier for; the certificate policy in full is on company verification.

Container weight, and the road limit at your end

Plywood is dense. A 40 ft container loaded to its roughly 26.7-tonne ship-line payload with 12 mm sheets fills only about 60% of its 67 cubic metres, so the weighbridge sets the sheet count, not the cube. We load every container to the lower of the ship-line payload and the road limit that applies at your end.

Tell us which limit that is when you enquire. If your inland leg runs under US highway rules, we cap the container at the same figure we use for the mainland — about 20 tonnes — and say so on the proforma invoice with the sheet count and the net and gross weights. The arithmetic is on the container weight guide.

Terms, documents, and checking us before you pay

Export quotes are issued in US dollars under Incoterms 2020, and always name the place or port: FCA Cochin where a sealed container is handed to your carrier, FOB Cochin where we deliver on board, CFR or CIF where agreed. Standard products ship against 50% advance with the balance before dispatch; custom specifications are 100% advance. Payment is by bank transfer (T/T).

Every shipment carries a commercial invoice, a packing list with bundle-wise net and gross weights, the Bill of Lading and a certificate of origin. Tell us your broker's checklist when you confirm the order: destination-specific documents are agreed before production ends, not after the container sails.

We ask for money before you have met us, so check us first. IEC AAJCC9689H is searchable at dgft.gov.in; RCMC/FIEO/10262/2024-2025 is our Federation of Indian Export Organisations registration for wood and articles; the LEI 98450010B744B73B1445 is on gleif.org if your bank runs a counterparty check. Each registration and the portal that confirms it is listed on company verification. Pre-shipment inspection is welcome, by you or an agency you appoint, and loading photographs and video of your container go out the same day it stuffs. The whole sequence is on how we handle an export order.
