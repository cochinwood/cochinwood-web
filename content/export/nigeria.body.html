The lane: Kochi to Apapa

Our group has made plywood in Perumbavoor, Kerala since 1986. The factory sits about 40 km from Cochin Port; containers are stuffed at the factory under our own supervision and gate in at the Vallarpadam terminal, so the inland leg is short and the sheet count in the box is the sheet count on the packing list.

West Africa is a different routing from the Gulf and East African lanes, and the container traffic we quote for Nigeria discharges at Apapa in Lagos. If your clearance runs through another terminal, say so in the enquiry and we will quote against that routing.

We do not publish sailing schedules. Port-to-port time depends on the carrier and the service, and a number printed on a page is wrong the first time the schedule changes. The sailing is confirmed alongside the quote, and the shipping documents are shared before the vessel sails.

The part of a Nigerian import we can actually control is the paperwork leaving our end, and the time to fix it is at enquiry. Tell us what your import requires before the proforma invoice is issued: a document that has to be named on the commercial invoice, a certificate tied to a particular mill, an inspection held before loading. Every one of those is cheap to arrange in week one and expensive to retrofit once a container is on the water. Nigeria is one of several lanes we serve; the export hub covers the rest.

What Nigerian buyers order

Three lines carry most of the volume: packing-grade plywood for crates and cases, commercial and BWR plywood for furniture and joinery, and film-faced shuttering plywood for formwork. Ready-made boxes and crates go on the same container for buyers who want packaging delivered rather than built.

Mixed loads are normal. A container split across two or three products is a standard order on this lane, not a special request, and both box sizes are worked: a 20 ft for smaller lots and weight-restricted haulage, a 40 ft where the road rules at your end allow the payload.

USD quotes on FOB Cochin terms

Export quotes are issued in US dollars, FOB Cochin by default. FCA at a named place is often the better term for a sealed container and we quote it on request. Standard products ship against 50% advance with the balance before dispatch; custom builds are 100% advance. Payment is by bank transfer (T/T).

We do not publish rate lists. Veneer and timber costs move and a printed number goes stale. Send the spec — thickness, sheet size, grade, face, quantity and discharge port — and a written quote comes back within one business day. What FOB Cochin does and does not include is set out in our note on the term, and the whole order path, from spec confirmation to the document set on sailing, is on how we export.

We ask for money in advance, so we make ourselves easy to check. Every registration we hold is listed with the government portal you can verify it on at company verification — the IEC, the FIEO RCMC, the LEI, the CIN and the GSTIN, all against the same legal entity that will invoice you.

ISPM-15, and what certification actually costs you if you ask late

Plywood panels are exempt from ISPM-15. Plywood is a processed wood product, pressed under heat and adhesive, so the panels ship without a heat-treatment stamp and without a fumigation certificate. The standard covers solid-wood packaging — pallets, crates, dunnage. Where we supply plywood boxes, crates or pallets, the ISPM-15 process and markings are handled on our side. If you run your own packing line, our note on heat treatment versus methyl bromide sets out the difference.

Product certification is a different question, and it is settled before the panels are pressed rather than after. If your import into Nigeria needs a particular certificate, test report or pre-shipment inspection, say so at enquiry: it decides which mill your order is placed with and it cannot be added once the goods exist. Each mill holds its own BIS licence, so there is no single number covering everything we sell; any certificate that applies to your order is named on the quotation by holder, number and product scope. Pre-shipment inspection by you or by any third-party agency you appoint is welcome, and we hold the goods for it.

What you see before the container sails

You should not have to wait for the doors to open in Lagos to learn what is inside. Three things close that gap, and all three are standard rather than chargeable extras: pre-shipment inspection by you or any third-party agency you appoint, with the goods held for it; photographs and video of your container as it loads, sent the same day; and a packing list carrying net and gross weight per bundle and sheet counts per pallet — the same numbers declared to the shipping line. Behind them sits the material guarantee: the panel that arrives matches the spec sheet confirmed on the proforma invoice.
