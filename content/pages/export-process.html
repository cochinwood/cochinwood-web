FCA or FOB

FCA [named place] is normally the right term for a sealed container. Delivery and risk transfer happen when the container is handed to the carrier you nominate — at our premises once we have loaded your collecting vehicle, or at a Cochin terminal once the goods are there at the carrier’s disposal. We clear the goods for export. You contract the main carriage.

FOB Cochin applies where we are responsible for delivering the goods on board the vessel you nominate. Risk transfers when the goods are on board, and we carry the cost to that point. You contract the main carriage here too. Many letters of credit still call for FOB, and we quote it on request.

Under Incoterms 2020 an FCA sale can still produce an on-board bill of lading: you instruct your carrier to issue one to us after loading. That has to be agreed in the booking, because the carrier is not obliged to do it.

Demurrage and detention are charged under the carriage, equipment and terminal contracts, not by the Incoterm. Who pays depends on who booked, the free time, and what caused the delay. The quotation states how origin delay, late vessel nomination, customs holds and late equipment return are allocated.

What you see before the container sails

You should not have to wait for the container doors to open at destination to know what is inside. Three things close that gap.

  • Pre-shipment inspection. Welcome, by you or any third-party agency you appoint. We hold the goods for inspection before loading.
  • Loading photos and video. Your container, your goods, shot at the door as it loads. Sent the same day.
  • Packing list with real weights. Net and gross weight per bundle and sheet counts per pallet — the same numbers declared to the shipping line.

Behind all three sits the material guarantee: the panel that arrives matches the approved spec sheet. Same thickness, same grade, same construction as confirmed on the proforma invoice.

Compliance: ISPM-15 and emissions

Three points export buyers ask about, answered straight.

  • ISPM-15 packing. All solid-wood packing — pallets, runners, bearers — is treated and stamped ISPM-15 compliant.
  • Plywood itself is exempt. Plywood is processed wood under ISPM-15, so the panels need no treatment stamp. Only the solid-wood packing around them does, and ours carries it.
  • E1 glue line. A low-emission E1 glue line is available on Okoume-faced panels for markets and end uses that require it.

Cores are rubberwood and eucalyptus; faces are Okoume or Gurjan. The species detail is on Woods We Use.

Common questions

What are Cochin Wood Industries' export payment terms?

Standard products: 50% advance with the order and balance before dispatch. Custom specifications: 100% advance with the order. Export quotes are in US dollars under Incoterms 2020, and always name the place or port: FCA Cochin where a sealed container is handed to your carrier, FOB Cochin where we deliver on board, CFR or CIF where agreed.

What export documents does Cochin Wood Industries provide?

Every shipment carries a commercial invoice, a packing list with net and gross weights and the Bill of Lading. A Certificate of Origin is included where the destination, bank or contract requires it. A treatment or fumigation certificate is supplied only where that treatment was actually carried out, and a phytosanitary certificate only where the commodity or destination rules call for one — for regulated wood packaging the authorised ISPM-15 mark is normally the evidence, not a separate certificate.

Is plywood subject to ISPM-15?

No. Plywood is processed wood and exempt under ISPM-15. Solid-wood packing such as pallets, runners and bearers does fall under the standard, and all solid-wood packing Cochin Wood Industries ships is treated and ISPM-15 stamped.

Can I inspect my plywood order before shipment?

Yes. Pre-shipment inspection is welcome, by the buyer or any appointed third-party agency, before loading at the factory 40 km from Cochin Port. Buyers also receive photos and video of their container as it loads, plus a packing list with net and gross weights.

How much plywood is loaded into a 40ft container?

Containers are loaded to the lower of the ship-line payload and the destination road limit, for example about 24.5 tonnes into a 40ft for Israel and about 20 tonnes for the USA, so containers clear destination weighbridges.
