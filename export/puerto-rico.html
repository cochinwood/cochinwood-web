Formaldehyde: confirm which regime your entry falls under

Buyers bringing plywood into Puerto Rico ask us the same certification question mainland buyers do. We will not tell you which regime applies to your entry — that is your customs broker's call, and it depends on the product type and its end use. What we will do is act on the answer.

If the answer is CARB or TSCA Title VI, name it at enquiry. It decides which mill your order is placed with, and it cannot be retrofitted after the panels are pressed. We make no blanket compliance claim: each mill holds its own BIS licence and its own test reports, and any certificate that applies to your order is identified on the quotation by holder, number and product scope, then released to verified buyers on request. A low-emission E1 glue line is available on Okoume-faced panels, but E1 and CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI are different regimes with different test methods, so confirm the exact class rather than treating one as a substitute. Our note on formaldehyde compliance for plywood exports sets out what to ask any supplier for; the certificate policy in full is on company verification.

Container weight, and the road limit at your end

Plywood is dense. A 40 ft container loaded to its roughly 26.7-tonne ship-line payload with 12 mm sheets fills only about 60% of its 67 cubic metres, so the weighbridge sets the sheet count, not the cube. We load every container to the lower of the ship-line payload and the road limit that applies at your end.

Tell us which limit that is when you enquire. If your inland leg runs under US highway rules, we cap the container at the same figure we use for the mainland — about 20 tonnes — and say so on the proforma invoice with the sheet count and the net and gross weights. The arithmetic is on the container weight guide.

Import essentials for Puerto Rico

What your customs broker needs to land a plywood shipment. We quote FCA or FOB Cochin in USD; duty and any tax at import are added on the destination side.

ItemDetail
HS codeHeading 4412 (the national item within 4412.31 / 4412.33 / 4412.34 / 4412.39 turns on panel construction and individual ply thickness as well as face species, and finished cases, pallets and drums are generally considered under 4415 — your broker confirms the line)
Customs dutyPuerto Rico sits inside US customs territory, so a direct India–Puerto Rico plywood shipment is dutied exactly like a mainland US entry, under HTS Chapter 4412. The base (Column 1/MFN) rate for the tropical hardwood-faced plywood typical of Indian commercial and marine ply runs 8% ad valorem on lines such as 4412.31, 4412.33, 4412.34 and 4412.51/.52 — India holds no US FTA or GSP preference, so 8% is the floor for this shipment, though a handful of coniferous-faced lines run lower and birch-faced panels are typically duty-free, and only your customs broker can confirm the exact 8/10-digit line. On top of the base rate: the Section 232 timber/lumber tariff (10% on softwood lumber, effective 14 Oct 2025) explicitly excludes HTS 4412 plywood, including lauan-faced panels, so it does not apply here, though Commerce is due to report to the President by 1 Oct 2026 on whether to extend Section 232 to hardwood products; and the IEEPA ‘reciprocal tariff’ on India, along with the Section 122 surcharge that briefly replaced it, are both no longer in force — the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on 20 Feb 2026, and the Section 122 surcharge lapsed by around 24 Jul 2026. A separate Section 301 action against forced labour, covering 60 economies including India, took effect 24 Jul 2026 at a 10% ad valorem surcharge on goods not covered by its exclusion list — but published sources disagree on whether wood products sit on that exclusion list, so whether the 10% reaches this shipment is unresolved; have your broker confirm the current Section 301 status of the specific HTS line before costing the order, since it is the difference between roughly 8% and roughly 18% total federal duty. Separately from the federal duty, and regardless of how the Section 301 question resolves, Puerto Rico’s own Impuesto sobre Ventas y Uso (IVU) applies to goods brought onto the island at a combined 11.5% (10.5% Commonwealth plus 1% municipal), administered by the Departamento de Hacienda via an Import Declaration (Model SC 2970) filed through the SURI portal on every shipment; this is the Puerto Rico buyer’s liability rather than CWI’s as FOB seller, and a buyer holding an Eligible Reseller Certificate can defer it and collect IVU on resale instead of paying it at the port.

Export quotes are issued in US dollars under Incoterms 2020, and always name the place or port: FCA Cochin where a sealed container is handed to your carrier, FOB Cochin where we deliver on board, CFR or CIF where agreed. FAQ

Do you supply CARB Phase 2 or TSCA Title VI certified plywood for Puerto Rico?

We make no blanket claim, and we will not tell you which regime your entry falls under - ask your customs broker. If the answer is CARB or TSCA Title VI, name it at enquiry: it decides which mill your order is placed with and cannot be added after the panels are pressed. The certificate that applies to your order is identified on the quotation by holder, number and product scope.

What HS code do you ship plywood to Puerto Rico under?

Plywood falls under HS heading 4412 (sub-headings 4412.31, 4412.33, 4412.34 and 4412.39 by face species); finished cases, pallets and drums are generally considered under 4415. We state the code we ship under on the commercial invoice, and your broker confirms the national tariff line and the rate in force.

Which bond grade suits an island climate?

MR is moisture-resistant, not waterproof. Where the panel will sit in damp air after it lands, specify BWR; where it will be wet, IS 710 BWP marine. We confirm the bond on the proforma invoice and every grade ships with its technical data sheet.

How heavy will you load the container?

To the lower of the ship-line payload, about 26.7 tonnes, and the road limit at your end. Tell us which limit applies on your inland leg when you enquire - if it runs under US highway rules we cap the container at about 20 tonnes, the same figure we use for the mainland, and state it on the proforma invoice with the sheet count.

Do you have a plywood factory or warehouse in Puerto Rico?

No — Cochin Wood is an Indian plywood factory near Cochin (Kochi), Kerala. We do not hold local stock or a warehouse in Puerto Rico; we ship full-container loads FOB Cochin to the discharge port you nominate in Puerto Rico. You buy factory-direct from India.
