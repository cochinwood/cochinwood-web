Biosecurity is the thing to get right first

Australia is one of the strictest destinations we ship to, and almost all of the risk sits in solid wood rather than in the panels.

Plywood panels are processed wood and exempt from ISPM-15: bare sheets carry no heat-treatment stamp and need none. Solid-wood packaging is the opposite case. Ours is heat-treated at registered facilities and stencilled with the IPPC mark, country code IN, treatment code HT and the registered facility number — the standard for exports to the EU, the UK and Australia. Methyl bromide fumigation is the other treatment recognised under ISPM-15, but it is being phased out by most destination countries, so we default to heat treatment unless you explicitly ask for MB.

Three practical points that decide whether a container is opened at the wharf:

  • Bark. Our port-rejection notes record bark residue on solid-wood components as a recurring rejection reason. Bark is prohibited regardless of treatment, so a compliant HT stamp does not save a member that still carries bark.
  • Repaired members. A crate whose replaced runner carries no visible IPPC mark is the mixed-timber failure that costs a full container offload and an inspection fee. Every solid-wood member has to carry its own mark, including any fitted after our yard.
  • Fumigation at destination. Where a destination calls for fumigation, the buyer specifies it and we arrange and bill it. It is not inside an FOB Cochin price. Wood packaging that arrives without a valid ISPM-15 mark is the usual trigger.

A phytosanitary certificate is issued where the destination requires one; Australia is among the destinations that commonly do. Ask your customs broker which of these applies to your consignment before the container is stuffed, not after it berths — the background is in our note on ISPM-15 stamp validity and re-stamping.

What we ship to Australia

On a voyage this long, weight and bond are the two specification decisions worth spending time on. For export crating to Australia the guidance in our own packing notes is BWR or BWP plywood with an okoume face: the lower density means a lighter crate, which matters when sea freight is rated on chargeable weight. Where crate gross weight reaches 1,500 kg, the cargo is irregular or top-heavy, or two-high stacking is expected in the hold, we spec a triple-wall build rather than reinforced single-wall.

Import essentials for Australia

What your customs broker needs to land a plywood shipment in Australia. We quote FOB Cochin in USD; duty and GST are added at import by your broker.

ItemDetail
HS code4412 (the item within 4412.31 / 4412.33 / 4412.34 / 4412.39 turns on panel construction and individual ply thickness as well as face species, and finished cases, pallets and drums are generally considered under 4415 — your broker confirms the line)
Customs dutyThe general (MFN) Australian customs duty for plywood under HS 4412 is 5% ad valorem under Schedule 3 of the Customs Tariff Act 1995, with a handful of narrow sub-lines — such as bamboo plywood and blockboard/laminboard/battenboard — already Free. Under the Australia–India Economic Cooperation and Trade Agreement (ECTA), in force since 29 December 2022, HS 4412 sits in Staging Category A in Australia’s schedule, meaning the tariff was eliminated on entry into force. Plywood from India that qualifies as originating under the ECTA Rules of Origin, and travels with a valid Certificate of Origin, therefore clears at 0% rather than the 5% general rate; without that certificate the 5% MFN rate applies instead. Ask your broker to confirm the exact 8-digit tariff line and that no separate anti-dumping or countervailing duty measure applies, since Australia runs active anti-dumping measures on plywood from some other origins.
GST at importGST at import is a flat 10%, with no reduced or exempt rate for plywood or other timber products. It is calculated on the Value of the Taxable Importation (VoTI) — the customs value of the goods, plus any customs duty payable, plus the cost of international transport and insurance to Australia — not on CIF value alone. This is the standard formula the Australian Border Force and Australian Taxation Office apply to all commercial imports, and GST is payable at the same time and in the same manner as customs duty, generally at clearance.
