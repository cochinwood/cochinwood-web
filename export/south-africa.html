The lane: Kochi to Durban

Our group has made plywood in Perumbavoor, Kerala since 1986. The factory sits about 40 km from Cochin Port; containers are stuffed at the factory under our own supervision and gate in at the Vallarpadam terminal, so the inland leg is short and the sheet count in the box is the sheet count on the packing list.

South Africa is a long-haul lane next to the Gulf runs we work every week, and the container traffic we quote for the market discharges at Durban. If you clear somewhere else, say so in the enquiry and we will quote against that routing.

We do not publish sailing schedules. Port-to-port time depends on the carrier and the service, and a number printed on a page is wrong the first time the schedule changes. The sailing is confirmed alongside the quote, and the shipping documents are shared before the vessel sails.

A long lane changes how you buy. Fuller, less frequent loads cost less per sheet in freight than topping up monthly, but they tie up more capital and leave less room to correct a specification between shipments. Tell us the run rate you are planning for and the quote is built around it rather than around a single box. South Africa is one of several lanes we serve; the export hub covers the rest.

Loading: plywood weighs out before it cubes out

Plywood is dense cargo. A container hits its permitted payload well before the space is full, so "how many sheets fit" is a weight question, not a volume one: thickness, panel density, pallet weight and the road limit at the discharge end all move the answer. Send us the road weight limit that applies on your side and the loading plan is built to it, so the box clears the weighbridge instead of being stripped at the gate. Every quote carries that plan — sheets per container, net and gross weight, loose-loaded and palletised where the difference matters. Our note on 20 ft loading by thickness works through the arithmetic.

ISPM-15, and what certification actually costs you if you ask late

Plywood panels are exempt from ISPM-15. Plywood is a processed wood product, pressed under heat and adhesive, so the panels ship without a heat-treatment stamp and without a fumigation certificate. The standard covers solid-wood packaging — pallets, crates, dunnage. Where we supply plywood boxes, crates or pallets, the ISPM-15 process and markings are handled on our side. If you run your own packing line, our note on heat treatment versus methyl bromide sets out the difference.

Product certification is a different question, and it is settled before the panels are pressed rather than after. If your import into South Africa needs a particular certificate, test report or pre-shipment inspection, say so at enquiry: it decides which mill your order is placed with and it cannot be added once the goods exist. Each mill holds its own BIS licence, so there is no single number covering everything we sell; any certificate that applies to your order is named on the quotation by holder, number and product scope. Pre-shipment inspection by you or by any third-party agency you appoint is welcome, and we hold the goods for it.

Import essentials for South Africa

What your clearing agent needs to land a plywood shipment in South Africa. We quote FCA or FOB Cochin in USD; customs duty and any import taxes are added at import, on the valuation basis your destination's customs law uses.

ItemDetail
HS code4412 (plywood; the national item within 4412.31 / 4412.33 / 4412.34 / 4412.39 turns on panel construction and individual ply thickness as well as face species, and finished cases, pallets and drums are generally considered under 4415 — your broker confirms the line)
Customs duty and import taxesSouth Africa applies its SACU-wide General customs duty of 10% ad valorem to plywood under HS 4412 — covering veneered panels, laminated wood, blockboard and battenboard alike — since India holds no free-trade or preferential-tariff agreement with SACU and none of the reduced-duty columns (EU/UK, EFTA, SADC, AfCFTA, MERCOSUR) apply to Indian-origin goods. No anti-dumping, countervailing or safeguard duty is currently in force on plywood from India or any other origin, so the 10% General rate is the only duty payable. Import VAT is 15%, charged not on the FOB or invoice value directly but on the Added Tax Value — customs value plus a 10% uplift plus the duty itself — which works out to roughly 28% of customs value for duty and VAT combined. Your clearing agent confirms the exact rate in force and the computed base at time of entry.
Landed-cost noteIndicative only — your broker must confirm the destination tariff line and the rates in force. Import VAT is not charged on CIF alone: the base is the customs value plus customs duty, and local law may add excise, fees and other incidental charges.
Conformity, standards and inspectionSouth Africa’s applicable standard for plywood is SANS 929, Plywood and composite board, covering materials, construction, preservative treatment, dimensions and performance. It is not enforced as an NRCS compulsory specification at import — unlike, for example, preservative-treated timber poles under VC 9092 — so plywood itself carries no mandatory Letter of Authority requirement; SANS 929 becomes effectively binding only where the panels are specified for a building-regulated structural or flooring use, since SANS 10400 (South Africa’s National Building Regulations) incorporates it by reference. FAQ

Which port do you ship to for South Africa?

Durban, unless you tell us otherwise. The discharge port is named on the quotation, and if you clear elsewhere we will quote against that routing. We ship full-container loads from Cochin; there is no local stock to draw on.

How long does a container take from Kochi to Durban?

It depends on the carrier and the service, and we would rather confirm it for your booking than print a figure the next schedule change makes wrong. The sailing is confirmed alongside the quote, and shipping documents are shared before the vessel sails.

What import duty will I pay on plywood in South Africa?

We do not state a rate for South Africa. We have never published one, and a wrong tariff is an expensive thing for a buyer to be handed. Duty and any import tax are set by the destination schedule against the line your goods are classified under, and applied at import. We state the HS code on the commercial invoice and issue the certificate of origin; your clearing agent confirms the line and the rate in force. We do not give customs rulings and will not promise a duty outcome.

Do you supply certification for import into South Africa?

Tell us what your import needs at enquiry, not after the goods are pressed. A certificate, test report or pre-shipment inspection decides which mill the order is placed with, and it cannot be added retrospectively. Each mill holds its own BIS licence; whatever applies to your order is named on the quotation by holder, number and product scope. Inspection by you or an agency you appoint is welcome and we hold the goods for it.

Do the plywood sheets need ISPM-15 treatment or fumigation for South Africa?

No. Plywood is a processed wood product and is exempt from ISPM-15, so the panels carry no heat-treatment stamp and need no fumigation certificate. The standard covers solid-wood packaging — pallets, crates, dunnage. Where we supply those, they are produced under an ISPM-15 compliant process with the marks and paperwork in order.

What are your payment terms for South Africa orders?

Standard products ship against 50% advance with the balance payable before dispatch; custom builds are 100% advance. We accept bank transfer (T/T), and all export invoicing is in US dollars. If you have not traded with us before, company verification lists every registration we hold and the government portal you can check it on.

What HS code is plywood under for South Africa imports?

Plywood falls under HS heading 4412 (sub-headings 4412.31, 4412.33, 4412.34 and 4412.39 by face species). We state the correct code on the commercial invoice for your clearing agent.

Do you have a plywood factory or warehouse in South Africa?

No — Cochin Wood is an Indian plywood factory near Cochin (Kochi), Kerala. We do not hold local stock or a warehouse in South Africa; we ship full-container loads FOB Cochin to Durban. You buy factory-direct from India.
